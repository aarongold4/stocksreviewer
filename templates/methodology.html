Methodology

How the numbers are made

Extraction

For each company we read the SEC's XBRL companyfacts data and pull annual figures from 10-K (and 20-F/40-F) filings. Because the same concept appears under different XBRL tags across companies and years — revenue alone spans several tags — each metric is resolved through a priority-ordered list of candidate tags, with older tags backfilling years the newer tag doesn't cover.

Fiscal years

Each annual figure is labeled by the calendar year of its reporting period's end date, not by the filing's stated fiscal-year field (which is unreliable for comparative-year figures pulled from later filings). This matches company convention for both September year-ends (e.g. a period ending Sept 2021 is FY2021) and January year-ends (a period ending Jan 2026 is FY2026). Restated values are superseded by the most recently filed version.

Derived metrics

Margins, returns (ROE, ROA), leverage (debt/equity, current ratio), per-share values (book value, FCF, revenue per share), and growth rates (3- and 5-year revenue CAGR) are computed from the extracted line items. Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capital expenditure. All of these are price-independent — they come entirely from filings.

Valuation model

The DCF estimate is a transparent two-stage model: the latest fiscal year's free cash flow is grown for five years at a rate clamped from the company's own revenue CAGR (capped between 0% and 15%), then at a 2.5% terminal rate, discounted at 10%, and divided by shares outstanding. It runs only when free cash flow and share count are positive. It is a deliberately simple sanity check, not a price target — every assumption is shown on the page so you can disagree with it.

Sector & industry classification

Companies are grouped using the GICS framework — the same 11 sectors Fidelity and most of the market use. Since EDGAR only provides each filer's SIC code (a government scheme), we map SIC codes to GICS sectors and industries with a curated crosswalk, plus company-level overrides for prominent names where SIC and GICS genuinely disagree (for example, Walmart is Consumer Staples under GICS but a "variety store" under SIC). Classification is therefore a close approximation, not an official GICS feed; a handful of smaller names may sit in an adjacent industry.

How current the data is

Prices update live — the chart is a TradingView embed. Fundamentals change only when a company files, so the dataset is rebuilt on a schedule rather than continuously: a weekly refresh pulls SEC's bulk XBRL archives (which themselves update within about a minute of each filing) and regenerates every page. Around earnings season you can run it more often. Each company page stamps the date its data was last extracted and links to the live EDGAR record, so you can always check freshness against the source.

Prices

Price charts are embedded from TradingView, which carries its own market-data licensing. StocksReviewer does not store or redistribute price data; price-based ratios like P/E are intentionally left to the live chart rather than baked into the static page.

Limits

XBRL tagging is imperfect; some companies (especially financials and foreign filers) tag items unusually, so a metric may be missing or occasionally wrong. Every page links to the original EDGAR filing so you can verify. If you find an error, it's in the extraction layer, not the SEC's data.
